How to build the Luma urine test strip reader

The complete parts list with specifications, where every component goes, and how to put it together. All sizes and positions come straight from the CAD model (build 20260913-163314).

Read this first. Luma Rev A is an engineering prototype, not a medical device or a finished product. The CAD reserves space for each purchased component (an “envelope”); the parts suggested here fit those envelopes, but the scan drive is still schematic, clamp hardware needs detailing, and no firmware is included yet. Treat this as a guide for building and testing a prototype.

Where everything goes

Positions are in millimetres from the enclosure’s own origin: x runs across (0 = centre, negative = left when you face the front), y runs up from the bottom of the case, and z runs forward from the back wall. The front panels sit at z ≈ 130 mm. Numbers match the part numbers in the tables and the CAD files.

Where does the Arduino go?

In the dry electronics compartment at the top of the enclosure, above the wet/dry barrier. The Uno stands upright against the back wall of the tray in the upper-left corner (x −89…−20, y 250…304, z 20…36 mm), with the fuse module to its right and the buzzer at the top right. Assembly, step by step

Follow the order in the engineering notes: sealed compartments first, then optics, handling, power, front and mount. Each step links to the 3D viewer with its parts highlighted.

1Prepare the parts

  1. Print the enclosure, trays and brackets (see printed parts). The main shell (01, 230 × 330 × 128 mm) is the largest print.
  2. Have the stainless-steel parts laser-cut and bent (see steel parts), and die-cut the EPDM gaskets and pads (see rubber parts).
  3. Melt four M4 heat-set inserts into the back wall of the shell at x ±78 mm, y 49 mm and y 281 mm. These carry the mounting plate.
  4. Program the Arduino and test every electronic module on the bench before it goes into the enclosure.

Check: Dry-fit the trays and cassettes in the shell before gluing or fastening anything.

2Fit the rear mounting interface

  1. Place the two rear standoffs (09, 10) vertically on the back of the shell (x −84…84, y 43…288, z −4…0 mm).
  2. Lay the universal mounting plate (08, 180 × 260 × 4 mm stainless) over them.
  3. Fix the stack with the four M4 captive screws (59) into the heat-set inserts at x ±78 mm, y 49 and 281 mm.
  4. Fit the tether eye (58) at the upper right of the back (x 69…85, y 280…296, z −12…−8 mm).

Check: The plate sits flat and the screws stay captive when undone. Every mount adapter uses the same four holes.

3Build the dry electronics compartment

  1. Fit the wet/dry barrier (38), a full-width horizontal shelf at y 219–222 mm. Everything above it stays dry.
  2. Seal the barrier and fit the dry electronics tray (11) above it against the back wall (x −98…98, y 224…320, z 12…78 mm). Leak-check this compartment before installing electronics.
  3. Arduino Uno (13): mount it upright on four 5 mm M3 standoffs on the tray’s back wall, in the upper-left corner (x −89…−20, y 250…304, z 20…36 mm), headers facing the front.
  4. Fit the fuse module (14) to the right of the Arduino (x −10…27, y 278…305, z 20…36 mm), the buzzer (19) at the top right (x 65…79, y 292…306, z 30…37 mm) and the strain relief (49) where cables leave at the top (x 58…76, y 305…317, z 25…30 mm).
  5. Mount the stepper driver and any small boards that aren’t modelled in the free area right of the fuse module (about x 30…95, y 225…290 mm). Then wire everything (see wiring) and close the lid (12).

Check: The USB port is still reachable, no wire is pinched by the lid, and the compartment passed its leak check.

4Assemble the optical module

  1. Fit the black optical chamber (22, x −64…94, y 145…204, z 22…88 mm) and its light baffle insert (24).
  2. Fix the strip datum carrier (25). It holds the test strip (26) flat at z 52 mm.
  3. Mount the scan rail (27) and M3 lead screw (36) along the top of the chamber, and the micro stepper (35) at the right end.
  4. Build the scan carriage (28): the TCS34725 sensor (29) in the centre facing the strip, and the two white LEDs (32, 33) on brackets 30 and 31, 38 mm apart, angled at the strip.
  5. Place the white reference tile (34) at the right end of the travel, level with the strip, and the home / jam sensor (54) beside it.
  6. Close the chamber with its removable lid (23).

The CAD puts the sensor face 14.5 mm above the pads; the engineering notes suggest testing 5–25 mm on a jig.

Check: With the lid closed, the sensor reads close to zero with the LEDs off (dark reading). The carriage reaches the reference tile at the end of its travel.

5Install strip handling and consumables

  1. Slide the sealed clean-strip cassette (20) and its lid (21) into the left column (x −106…−72, y 87…217, z 22…67 mm).
  2. Fit the sample drawer (37) behind the input shutter, with the drip tray (39) underneath it.
  3. Insert the used-strip waste cartridge (40) at the bottom (x −66…76, y 16…72, z 17…110 mm), with its transport lid (41) and closure slide (42).
  4. Fit the cleaning-fluid cartridge (43) at the bottom right (x 81…105, y 20…80, z 25…73 mm).
  5. Mount the cartridge-present sensor (52) beside the waste cartridge and the drip-overflow sensor (53) at the drip tray.

Check: Each cassette, tray and cartridge comes out from the front without exposing the clean strips. Prove the motion and jam recovery without samples before any fluid testing.

6Connect the power

DC version: mount the DC jack (47) on its bracket (46) at the upper right (x 66…86, y 230…246, z 93…109 mm), with the cable gland (48) beside it. Run the supply through the reverse-polarity diode and fuse module (14) to the Arduino and motor driver.

Battery version: fit the battery tray (44) in front of the dry-compartment lid (x −91…−10, y 228…257, z 82…129 mm) and place the protected 2S pack (45) in it. Show the battery version ↗

Route the harness down the cable channel (50) on the right side (x 99…107, y 114…302, z 103…110 mm), clipped above any area that can hold liquid.

Check: Polarity, fuse values and the 5 V rail are checked with a multimeter before the Arduino is connected.

7Fit the front panels and door

  1. Fit the EPDM gasket (07) to the door frame (02), and hang the frame on the two hinges (55, 56) on the left edge.
  2. Mount the display module behind the 84 × 40 mm opening in the upper front panel (03), then fit the polycarbonate window (16), bezel (15), status LED (18) and QR label (17).
  3. Fit the optics front cover (05), the lower front panel (04) and the input shutter (06).
  4. Fit the door interlock switch (51) at the upper right (x 99…109, y 284…298, z 118…125 mm) and the service lock (57) on the right edge (x 98…110, y 158…170, z 114…127 mm).

Check: The door closes evenly on the gasket, the interlock switches when it opens, and the lock holds it shut.

8Choose and attach a mount

Pick the mount that suits the site. Every adapter is a 180 × 260 × 4 mm stainless plate that bolts to the universal plate through the same four holes. Then fit the mount-specific parts (see mount kits):

Check: Do a site survey before choosing a mount, and proof-test the installation off-site before public use (see ENGINEERING.md).

Power path

DC jack (47) or battery pack (45) → Schottky diode → fuse module (14): F1 feeds the Arduino’s VIN and F2 feeds the stepper driver’s motor supply. The Arduino’s 5 V rail powers the sensor, LCD, LEDs and switches. A 2S pack falls below the Uno’s recommended 7 V near the end of its charge: stop at about 7 V (read on A2), or add a 5 V regulator.

First power-up and testing

  1. Before power: check continuity, polarity and fuse values with a multimeter.
  2. Power up with the motor unplugged and check the 5 V rail.
  3. Run an I²C scanner sketch: the sensor should appear at 0x29 and the LCD at 0x27 or 0x3F.
  4. Close the optics lid and take a dark reading with the LEDs off; it should be close to zero.
  5. Read the white reference tile (34) repeatedly and check the readings are repeatable.
  6. Connect the motor and set the driver’s current limit. Check homing, full travel and jam recovery without samples.
  7. Check the interlocks: opening the door stops motion, a missing or full cartridge blocks testing, and an overflow stops sample intake.
  8. Only then move on to wet tests, and calibrate for each pad and strip supplier.

Servicing

  1. Disable motion.
  2. Close the waste cartridge slide (42), then withdraw and replace the waste cartridge (40). Never empty it through the clean compartment.
  3. Remove and wash or replace the wetted insert, and inspect the seals.
  4. Refill the clean strips through the clean access path (20).
  5. Replace the protected battery if fitted.
  6. Close and lock the covers, then run the reference and sensor checks.
